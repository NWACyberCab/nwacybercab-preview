How this number is calculated

Method: projection, not official Tesla data. We start from Tesla's last stated total ({{ fsdBaseText }} unsupervised miles on {{ fsdBaseDateText }}) and add the miles the driverless fleet would cover since then: fleet size × miles per car per day, summed second by second. Fleet size is drawn straight between public fleet-tracker counts (256 driverless cars on Sept 3; about 500 on Oct 14: Austin ~400, Dallas 68, Houston 33), then assumed to keep growing by about 6 cars a day (the Sept–Oct average) for 8 weeks and then hold steady. Miles per car per day is our assumption: {{ fsdMpdBase }} at the Sept 3 fleet size, rising as the fleet grows (bigger fleets keep each car busier) up to a cap of {{ fsdMpdCap }}. Right now that's about {{ fsdFleetText }} cars at {{ fsdMpdText }} miles a day each. Tracker counts are cars seen in service, not official, and real utilization may be lower or higher, so treat this as an estimate. It covers Tesla's own driverless fleet (Bay Area safety-driver cars excluded) and resets to Tesla's next official total. RoboRide is not yet operating, and none of these are RoboRide miles.

Latest updates

Cybercab & Robotaxi news.

Industry news from independent reporting, as of October 19, 2026. These are Tesla developments, not RoboRide announcements; RoboRide is not affiliated with Tesla.

  • Coming Oct 21Tesla reports third-quarter 2026 results after the market closes on Oct 21, with a live Q&A webcast at 4:30 p.m. Central. Teslarati notes the call is a natural venue for updated fleet numbers and a Dallas date. (Tesla via Nasdaq · Teslarati)
  • Oct 17, 2026Tesla extended Austin Robotaxi hours again: rides now run from 6 a.m. to midnight, up from 11 p.m. earlier this month. Tesla’s SI lead has said FSD v15 is meant to unlock round-the-clock service, with a rollout to the Robotaxi fleet targeted for October. (Not a Tesla App)
  • Oct 15, 2026NHTSA’s latest crash-report data adds two more low-speed Robotaxi crashes from August, in Dallas and Austin, where a remote operator rather than the self-driving system was in control. Both were property damage only, at about 1 mph or less, with no passengers aboard. Tesla now has 36 Robotaxi incident reports on file. (Electrek)
  • Oct 13, 2026NHTSA is reviewing a petition (DP26010, opened Oct 9) alleging Tesla robotaxis, both Model Y and Cybercab, exceeded active school-zone speed limits. It is not yet a formal investigation. (The Verge · Automotive News)
  • Oct 13, 2026A Tesla UI engineer says on-demand rear and side camera views for Cybercab riders are “in development” (no date). Tesla’s Cybercab chief engineer also said new seats are coming. (Not a Tesla App · EVBytes)
  • Oct 10, 2026Tesla says 300+ Cybercabs are serving Austin, and its SI lead says Dallas is next (“Dallas soon,” no date given). On Oct 8, Texas’s vehicle registry showed Tesla’s Cybercab count jump from 169 to 319. (Teslarati · CultureMap Dallas · TeslaNorth)
  • Oct 2026NHTSA is reviewing how Tesla certified the Cybercab to federal safety standards, including how it handles mirrors and rear visibility. Tesla got a 30-day extension to respond, and reports put the deadline for its sworn answers at Oct 30. (Teslarati · TeslaNorth · Not a Tesla App)
  • Sept 3–4, 2026At its Austin Cybercab event Tesla announced 1 million unsupervised Robotaxi miles; public Cybercab rides in Austin opened the next day. (TeslAnt · All Things Elon)
  • July 22, 2026On its Q2 call Tesla reported 380,000+ unsupervised Robotaxi miles across six cities in Texas and Florida. (Business Insider)
FAQ

Common questions.

Is RoboRide operating yet?

Not yet. RoboRide LLC is preparing to launch and no cities are live today. Join the waitlist to hear when service starts in your area.

Where will RoboRide operate?

Northwest Arkansas. The planned service area includes Bentonville, Rogers, Springdale, Fayetteville, Farmington, Siloam Springs, and Eureka Springs. We plan to launch city by city.

What services will you offer?

Two things: driverless rides for people, and local delivery of goods for businesses and customers, both using autonomous Tesla Cybercabs.

How much will it cost?

Pricing hasn't been set yet. Rates will be announced before launch, and waitlist members will hear first.

Is RoboRide part of Tesla?

No. RoboRide LLC is an independent Northwest Arkansas company that plans to operate Tesla Cybercab vehicles. We are not affiliated with or endorsed by Tesla, Inc.

I run a business. Can we work together?

We'd like to hear from you. Email partners@nwaroboride.org, or choose “Business partnership” on the waitlist form, and we'll follow up about delivery, employee rides, or venue pickup zones.

What happens to my email if I join the waitlist?

We use it only to contact you about RoboRide, and we will never sell it. See our Privacy Policy for details.

How do I reach rider support?

Email support@nwaroboride.org with questions now. Once rides are live, the same address will handle ride support.
