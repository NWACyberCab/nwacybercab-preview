About RoboRide

Built in NWA,
for NWA.

RoboRide LLC is an independent Northwest Arkansas company preparing to operate a fleet of autonomous Tesla Cybercabs for rides and goods delivery. We aren't operating yet; this page explains what we're building and how we plan to get there. Founded June 8, 2026.

Mission

Safe, simple, driverless transportation for Northwest Arkansas.

Make getting around NWA, and getting things across it, easier, by running a local autonomous fleet that people and businesses can count on.

Vision

A region where a ride or a delivery is always a tap away.

From the University of Arkansas to XNA to Bentonville Square, with less traffic stress, no tailpipe emissions, and a local team behind the service.

What we believe

Our principles.

Safety before speed

We'll launch only with the approvals state law requires, and expand only when operations are ready.

Honest by default

No made-up numbers. If something isn't decided, such as pricing or launch timing, we'll say so.

Local first

NWA is home. We want our service to fit how this region actually moves.

Privacy matters

We'll never sell your data. See our Privacy Policy.

Founder

Who's behind RoboRide.

Joseph Bowers, Founder

Joseph Bowers is the founder of RoboRide LLC. Born and raised in Fayetteville, he's an Arkansas native who drives a Tesla with Full Self-Driving and has seen up close what autonomous driving can do. At 23, he started RoboRide to bring safe, driverless transportation to his home region and to help make Northwest Arkansas roads safer for everyone.

Launch roadmap

How we get to launch.

These milestones depend on vehicle availability, state approval and readiness, so they aren't dated. Each one happens only when the one before it is complete.

  1. Build interest Now

    Gather riders and businesses on the waitlist so we know where demand is strongest.

  2. Secure vehicles

    Acquire Cybercabs if and when they become available to independent operators like RoboRide.

  3. State approval

    Apply to the Arkansas State Highway Commission for an approved autonomous vehicle program. How this works

  4. Supervised start

    Operate with a human operator on board for the first six months, as state rules require.

  5. Driverless service in a first city

    Apply to remove the on-board operator requirement, then open driverless rides and deliveries in one NWA city.

  6. Grow city by city

    Expand across the planned service area as operations prove out.
